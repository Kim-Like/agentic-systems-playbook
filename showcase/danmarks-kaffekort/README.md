# Danmarks Kaffekort: a curated specialty-coffee map as a content-growth feature

**Live:** [thirdwave.dk/kaffekort](https://thirdwave.dk/kaffekort)
**Platform:** [lavprishjemmeside.dk](https://lavprishjemmeside.dk)

Danmarks Kaffekort is an interactive map of Denmark's best specialty coffee bars, from La Cabra
in Aarhus to Prolog in Copenhagen, curated by coffee quality rather than by whoever paid for a
pin. I built it as a content-growth feature for a Danish coffee site: a page people actually
want, that also happens to do real work for search. This is the case study for how a
hand-curated, quality-filtered map beats a generic map embed, for the visitor and for the site.

## The problem a coffee map actually solves

A coffee enthusiast in Denmark does not want "cafes near me." They want **good** cafes near
them: specialty roasters, third-wave bars, the places worth a detour. A default map gives you
every coffee-serving business in a five-kilometer radius, ranked by foot traffic and ad spend.
That is noise to someone who cares about the cup. The job the map has to do is **curation**:
this list is handpicked on coffee quality, organized so you can scan a city and trust every pin.

## What it does for the people who use it

- **Find specialty coffee by city, not by accident.** The map is organized around Denmark's
  coffee cities, so you browse Aarhus or Copenhagen as a place, not as a cluster of pins.
- **Every entry is curated.** Inclusion is a judgment about quality, which is the entire value.
  A trusted shortlist is worth more than an exhaustive directory.
- **It is built to explore.** Illustrated, zoomable city views make it something you browse for
  pleasure, not a utility you bounce off. People stay, and they come back before a trip.

## Why this is good for the site, not just the visitor

This is the part worth stealing if you run a content site. A genuinely useful, curated
interactive page is one of the strongest organic-growth assets you can own:

- **It targets real local intent.** People search for specialty coffee in a specific Danish
  city. A curated, city-organized map is the page that deserves to answer that query, and it is
  defensible because the curation cannot be auto-generated.
- **It earns dwell time and return visits.** An explorable map keeps people on the page and
  brings them back before they travel. Engagement signals follow.
- **It is shareable and linkable.** "Here is the map of the best coffee in Denmark" is a thing
  people send to a friend and other sites link to. That is how a page earns authority honestly.
- **It compounds.** Add a city, add a venue, and the same asset keeps growing in coverage and
  in the queries it can answer, without rebuilding anything.

A generic third-party map embed gives you none of this. It is someone else's data, someone
else's ranking, no curation, and nothing for search to reward. The curation is the moat.

## The method (transferable to any content site)

You do not need this exact subject. The pattern is "a curated interactive map as a
content-growth feature," and it works for any domain where **quality beats proximity** and a
local audience is searching by place: independent bookshops, natural-wine bars, climbing gyms,
record stores.

1. **Curate ruthlessly, on one clear quality bar.** The list's value is what you leave off.
   Write down the inclusion rule so it stays consistent as the map grows.
2. **Organize around places people search for.** City or region first. People think in
   destinations; match that, and you match their queries.
3. **Make it worth exploring, not just querying.** A map you enjoy browsing earns the dwell
   time and the return visit that a lookup tool never will.
4. **Own the data.** Curation you control is an asset that compounds and cannot be commoditized.
   A third-party embed is a feature you rent and cannot rank with.
5. **Let it grow in place.** Adding coverage should extend the same asset, never trigger a
   rebuild. Structure it so more venues and more cities are additive.

## Result

A page Danish coffee people actually use to find the good stuff, that gives the site a curated,
explorable, genuinely-linkable asset built to grow. See it live at
[thirdwave.dk/kaffekort](https://thirdwave.dk/kaffekort).
